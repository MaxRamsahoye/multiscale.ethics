Multi-Scale Ethics
The paradigm the article proposes: rethinking ethics for the grand dimensions of global civilisation, by asking how normative theories interact across scales – from the individual, through the collective, to civilisation as a whole.
Hybrid Ethics Stack
The article’s synthesis: the ethics of civilisation as layered theories, each operating at its own scale, together producing a higher-order ethical synergy that none could achieve alone.
Classes of Agent
The individual, the collective and the civilisational: kinds of agent, each with its own characteristic ethics, whose meeting points give rise to many of our hardest moral conflicts.
Complicated and Complex
A complicated system has many parts but can, with enough effort, be fully specified; a complex system is adaptive, non-linear and emergent, changing in response to the very rules applied to it.
Normative Ethics
The branch of moral philosophy concerned with how we ought to act. Its three major theories are consequentialism, deontology and virtue ethics.
Consequentialism
The theory that an action is right if it produces the best overall consequences. In the article, the ethics of the state: system-wide optimisation of aggregate welfare.
Utilitarianism
The best-known form of consequentialism, associated with Jeremy Bentham and John Stuart Mill: act so as to bring about the greatest happiness or well-being of the greatest number.
Deontology
The theory that an action is right if it conforms to a moral duty or rule, regardless of its consequences. In the article, the ethics of the city: shared rules that let strangers trust one another.
Categorical Imperative
Kant’s principle of duty: act only on rules you could will to be universal laws, and treat people always as ends in themselves, never merely as means.
Virtue Ethics
The theory, rooted in Aristotle, that morality is a matter of cultivating good character rather than following rules or calculating outcomes. In the article, the ethics of the tribe, where character can be known first-hand.
Alienation
The objection, pressed by Bernard Williams and Peter Railton, that impartial calculation estranges a person from their own relationships and commitments – consequentialism applied at the scale of the individual.
Cultural Evolution
Change in a population’s beliefs, norms and practices over time through variation, transmission and selection, much as in biological evolution.
Memetic Adaptation
An idea – here a normative one – that spreads and persists because it confers an advantage in a particular environment. The article reads each ethical theory as a memetic adaptation to a scale of society.
Multilevel Selection
The view that selection acts at several levels at once – on individuals within groups, and on groups themselves – summed up by David Sloan Wilson and E. O. Wilson: ‘Selfishness beats altruism within groups. Altruistic groups beat selfish groups.’
Dunbar’s Number
The cognitive limit of roughly 150 people with whom one person can maintain stable relationships; in the article, the threshold beyond which virtue alone can no longer regulate a society.
Convergent Cultural Evolution
The tendency, as civilisation integrates, for different cultures and their ethics to arrive at shared values; in the article, the route by which an aligned Singleton could emerge.
Moral Convergence
The argument that, as societies grow, virtue and duty increasingly answer to outcomes, and that the best versions of the major theories converge – what Derek Parfit called ‘climbing the same mountain on different sides’.
Singleton
Nick Bostrom’s term for a world order with a single decision-making agency at the highest level. It need not be monolithic, and could be formed through convergent evolution rather than coercion.
Holarchy
A hierarchy of holons – wholes that are at once parts of larger wholes. In the article, the nested, plural and adaptive structure of an aligned Singleton.
Autopoiesis
The self-production and self-maintenance of a living or complex system – a body, a social body, a civilisation.
Autopoethics
Anna Riedl’s portmanteau of autopoiesis and ethics: ethics understood as part of how a complex system reproduces itself.
Multipolar Trap
A situation in which many actors, each acting rationally or dutifully by its own lights, together produce an outcome that none of them would choose – what Scott Alexander called ‘Moloch’.
Leviathan
Hobbes’s image of the sovereign state; in the article, the moral outlook of governing at the scale of the state.
Seeing Like a State
James C. Scott’s phrase for the simplifying, aggregate view that states take of the societies they govern.
